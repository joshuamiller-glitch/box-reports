## 1. Inputs (all from D7's own tape; nothing else)
* Trade print stream of the traded NQ contract: (exchange timestamp with at least 1-s resolution, price). Every print counts (no size filter, no side filter). Prices in the RAW contract frame (the research used a back-adjusted frame; only differences matter, see section 6).
* The base signals (each book's entry decision): second of the decision, side (+1 long / -1 short), entry price E (the price the base would use for the signal, as D7 logs it), target distance td (points), stop distance (points), book, session.
* Clock: exchange wall-clock UTC seconds. 5-minute bars are aligned to epoch multiples of 300 s; because Eastern Time differs from UTC by whole hours, these are the same as :00, :05, ..., :55 in Eastern Time. No time-zone logic is needed in the computation; log both UTC and ET.

## 2. Definition (frozen; every rule is exact)
1. **Seconds grid.** Work on 1-second bins [s, s+1) of print time. For each bin keep hi(s) = highest print price, lo(s) = lowest print price; a bin with no print does not exist (no state change).
2. **5-minute bars.** A bar is the set of bins with floor(s/300) equal; bar high = max of hi(s), bar low = min of lo(s). Only bars containing at least one print exist; consecutive existing bars are treated as adjacent whatever the time gap (no contiguity requirement).
3. **Swing (fractal-5).** Bar j is a swing HIGH if its high is strictly greater than the highs of bars j-2, j-1, j+1, j+2 (existing bars, in order); a swing LOW mirrors with lows (strictly lower). The swing is KNOWN from the first second at or after the end of bar j+2 (i.e. s >= (floor(s_bar_j+2 start/300)+1)*300).
4. **Ring of 3.** Keep at most the 3 most recent KNOWN swing highs and the 3 most recent KNOWN swing lows (price L, known time). When a 4th newer swing of the same kind becomes known, drop the oldest; a dropped swing can no longer break.
5. **Break and flip level (direction is part of the rule).** A swing HIGH L is BROKEN at the first bin s >= its known time with hi(s) >= L + 10.0; a swing LOW L is broken at the first bin with lo(s) <= L - 10.0 (prices in points; a print exactly at L +- 10.0 counts). At the break bin create a FLIP LEVEL at price L with break time s_b, remove the swing from the ring. A flip level created from a swing HIGH broken upward is SUPPORT type (dir +1); from a swing LOW broken downward it is RESISTANCE type (dir -1). **A base signal is only affected by flip levels of the OPPOSITE type to its side: a LONG counts RESISTANCE flip levels (levels from swing lows broken down) that lie ABOVE the entry; a SHORT counts SUPPORT flip levels (swing highs broken up) that lie BELOW the entry.** (The earlier one-pager wording 'both types count' was imprecise; the research mask and S4b's clean-room mask use this direction rule. A flip level of the same type as the trade, e.g. a support flip above a long, is ignored.)
6. **Validity and freshness.** A flip level is valid for bins s with s_b <= s < s_b + 14400 (strictly less than 4 h). It is FRESH at bin s unless it has been touched: it is touched at the first bin s' > s_b (the bin after the break bin) such that the level has been 'away' (an earlier bin s'' with s_b < s'' < s' with lo(s'') > L + 0.5 or hi(s'') < L - 0.5) AND lo(s') <= L <= hi(s'). Once touched it is stale for good. (At the break the price is already 10 pts beyond L, so 'away' is true from the first later bin in practice; implement the 0.5 test anyway.)
7. **Flag at a base signal** at bin s_sig (the signal second; the state used is the state AFTER processing bin s_sig, i.e. a break in s_sig counts, a touch in s_sig makes the level stale): the signal is FLAGGED (bt_flag_D40 = 1) if there is a valid, fresh flip level of the opposite type with d = (L - E) for a long, (E - L) for a short satisfying 0 <= d <= min(40.0, td). bt_flag_D20 uses 20.0. d = 0 counts. Shadow action: would_skip = bt_flag_D40.
8. **Order of evaluation inside every bin s** (this order is the unit-test contract): (a) append the print(s) to bin s (hi, lo); (b) if s is the first bin at or after a bar close, finalize that bar and update swings (rule 3) and the ring (rule 4) BEFORE looking at bin s for breaks; (c) for every swing in the ring with known time <= s: test the break (rule 5) with hi(s)/lo(s); create flip levels; (d) for every flip level created in an earlier bin: update 'away' and test the touch with bin s (rule 6); (e) expire flip levels with s - s_b >= 14400; (f) then, and only then, evaluate any base signal whose decision second is s (rule 7) and write its log row.
9. **Per book**: every base signal of the six core books (KB PE SM HM MV PB; U7/K7/H7/P7/M7/A naming as D7 uses) is evaluated identically; X1-X5 cells and other overlays are not evaluated.

## 3. State and cost
* Bars: only the last 5 bars (O(1)). Swing ring: 3 highs + 3 lows. Flip levels: at most about 6 per hour (research: 786 flip events per month at 5 min = 1.1 per hour on average, 4 h life) so under 20 live at any time. Per bin: O(ring + flip levels) <= 30 comparisons. Memory: a few KB. No look-back arrays, no disk.
* The 1-s evaluation grid is natural: process each second once, in the order of rule 8.

## 4. Log (forward_register/bt_flip_shadow.csv; schema in FORWARD_SHADOW_ZONES_SPEC.md; check script code/zones3/bt_forward_check.py)
One row per base signal at the signal second: ts_signal_utc, ts_signal_et, book, session, side (+1/-1), entry_price, target_dist_pts, stop_dist_pts, bt_flag_D40, bt_flag_D20, would_skip, n_fresh_flip_levels_ahead (opposite-type fresh levels with 0 <= d <= td), nearest_flip_price (the flagging level with smallest d), nearest_flip_dist_pts (= d), nearest_flip_age_s (= s_sig - s_b), nearest_flip_dir (support/resistance), nearest_flip_break_size_pts (max extension beyond L up to s_sig), nearest_flip_swing_age_s (s_b - known time of the swing), n_flip_levels_total_last_4h, n_flip_levels_behind_within_stop, bwt_flag, bwt_nearest_dist_pts, bwt_nearest_age_s, bwt_n_levels_behind (bwt_flag = a fresh flip level of the SAME type as the trade lies behind the entry, between entry and stop, within min(40, stop distance); logged only, tested as VOLUME/DEAD), trade_taken (0/1), base_net_dollars (filled at the trade's close), exit_type (stop/target/th_l2_cut/lock/other), lib_net_pts (library 60/60 net pts if available), result_ts_utc.
Also, as a second file, one row per flip-level creation (bt_flip_events.csv: break_ts_utc, price L, kind, swing_known_ts_utc, ext_at_flag) so the live detector can be compared with the stream rebuilt from history (expected agreement >= 95% of events; residual differences come from tick/second-level data differences, see S4b repro).

## 5. Roll handling and gaps
* The research used a back-adjusted continuous price path; live prices are the raw contract. All rules use price DIFFERENCES within one contract, so raw works. At a contract roll (the book switches contract) clear all state (bars, ring, flip levels) and rebuild from the new contract's prints; expect at most about 4 rolls a year and a few hours of reduced coverage. The research did not clear state at rolls (the path is continuous): the effect is a handful of signals per roll, ignored.
* Maintenance breaks and holidays: no prints, no bins, no state change; bar adjacency ignores gaps (rule 2); validity (rule 6) is wall-clock 4 h.
* Do not backfill state across a D7 restart longer than 4 h; after a restart warm up with the last 4 h of prints (replayed through rule 8) before the first signal.
