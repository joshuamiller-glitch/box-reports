# Algo priority: MANAGED EXITS ON THE LOSS SIDE first, smart trailing on the profit side second (order from Josh, relayed)

Josh, 27 Nov 14:21Z, verbatim: "the key is managed exits on loss side and maybe smart trailing on profit side"

(relay) My reading. Sets the order of work inside the algo build and in all three profiles (big-target books, short book targets, negative-R). Research only.

1. LOSS SIDE IS THE MAIN JOB. The loop's first purpose: a trade that is going to lose is closed for less than the back-up stop, and a trade that is only dipping is left alone. So the central measurement, per book, on the exact engine: of the trades that ended at the full stop under the reference, how many did the loop get out of earlier and how much did that save; of the trades the loop closed on the loss side, how many would have recovered (to breakeven, to target) and what that cost. Net saving per trade, max DD, worst week, worst day. Loop A's first rarity check showed its leave and cut rules closing trades that then did better: that discrimination is THE problem to solve; say for each version how it changed.
2. WHERE TO LOOK FOR DISCRIMINATION (loss side), in this order: (a) the condition of the trade itself when under water (how fast it got there, time under water, whether it ever worked, distance to the back-up stop); (b) what is between price and the stop: real resting size and its age, is it being eaten or pulled, a stop-run-and-reclaim versus acceptance (L3); (c) flow: who is aggressive, absorption on our side or theirs; (d) ES / RTY / leaders moving with the loss or not; (e) options flow turning; (f) the book's own signal: has the reason for the entry gone (opposite signal, trigger line lost). Use the event-level moment test (13:01Z item 3a) to rank these, then build the rule; combinations expected.
3. ACTIONS ON THE LOSS SIDE to compare: leave all; leave half and hold half; tighten the back-up stop to just beyond the protecting size; hold through a detected stop-run; leave and allow a re-entry if the signal re-fires (with the re-entry rows L0-L3).
4. PROFIT SIDE SECOND, AND ONLY TRAILING: no fixed target; once in profit, a trail whose distance and style follow the landscape (structure when orderly, wider when the wall ahead is being eaten and ES confirms, tighter on exhaustion or a real wall holding); partials only if the trail test shows they add. Compare against the reference target. Keep it simple until the loss side works.
5. REPORTING: every ALGO line to Josh leads with the loss side: full stops avoided, cost of false exits, net, drawdown and worst week versus the live mix.
