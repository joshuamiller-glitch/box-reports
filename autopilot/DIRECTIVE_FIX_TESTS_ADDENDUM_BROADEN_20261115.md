PART C — NEW WAYS TO USE A CHECKLIST (so far it was only take / skip).
C1. WAIT FOR IT. When a book signals and the checklist is not yet satisfied, hold the signal open for N minutes (5 / 15 / 30 / 60) and enter when the checklist completes, at market or on a pullback to the signal price. Compare with skipping and with entering at once. A discretionary trader waits; the engine has only skipped.
C2. STAND DOWN AFTER A REJECTION. When a signal is rejected, block that book and side for N minutes (or until the rejecting conditions clear) instead of taking the next signal immediately. Compare: next-qualified, stand-down, and A1's gate-every-signal.
C3. BETTER PRICE. For an accepted signal, enter with a resting order at the nearest level cluster or VWAP band on the favourable side within k points (k = 3 / 5 / 10), cancel after N minutes; report fill rate, price gained and the trades missed.
C4. THE CHECKLIST AS THE SIGNAL. Books whose entry IS the confluence: enter when at least k independent clusters agree on a side with no book signal needed, per session; one entry per side per session window; standard and forecast-scaled brackets. Through the injection hook, with the planted-edge and shuffle controls.
C5. LIVE RE-SCORING IN THE TRADE. Re-score the checklist every minute while a trade is open: score falls below a floor -> cut or tighten; score rises -> hold through the cut, lock later, or add. Jointly with the existing loss and profit lines, never on a base with them removed.
C6. FADE STRONG DISAGREEMENT. When a book signals and the independent clusters agree strongly AGAINST it: skip, and separately test taking the opposite side. Expect this to be rare; report the count before the result.
C7. CROSS-BOOK AGREEMENT. Other books' state as conditions: another book already in on the same side and in profit / in loss; two books signalling the same side within N minutes; books disagreeing. Size and skip versions.

PART D — RICHER CONDITIONS (so far every condition was yes / no at one instant).
D1. STRENGTH, NOT JUST STATE. Each condition also as a graded value (how far beyond its threshold, as a z-score against its own history at that clock); scores built from summed strengths as well as counts.
D2. SEQUENCE AND FRESHNESS. The ORDER in which conditions became true and how long ago: setup -> trigger -> confirmation chains (e.g. level sweep, then structure break, then retest; squeeze on, then fired, then first pullback); minutes since each condition turned true; conditions that just turned true versus ones true all session. Test whether fresh agreement beats stale agreement.
D3. PERSISTENCE AND DECAY. For each condition, how long its edge lasts after it turns true (1 / 5 / 15 / 60 min). Sets the window inside which a checklist pass stays valid.
D4. MULTI-TIMEFRAME ALIGNMENT AS ITS OWN SCORE. For each indicator family, the count of timeframes in agreement and whether the HIGHEST timeframe agrees; conflict between higher and lower timeframe as an explicit veto condition.
D5. SIDE AND REGIME SPLITS. Separate checklists for long and short; for the chop call and the trend call; for high and low expected move; for fresh and rechase context; for event and non-event days. A condition can be a "go" in one regime and a veto in another: allow that, but only where the shuffle bar for the split cell is beaten.
D6. WHAT EACH CONDITION PREDICTS. Score every condition against FOUR outcomes separately, not only net P&L: maximum favourable move (how far it goes), maximum adverse move (how much heat it takes), time to target, and probability of reaching 1R before -1R. Publish the four tables. This is the bridge to Part F.

PART E — MAKING SMALL CELLS MEASURABLE (the reason "nothing passes" in single cells is often sample size).
E1. PARTIAL POOLING. Estimate each condition's effect per book x session with shrinkage toward its session-wide and book-wide effect (hierarchical model). Report raw and shrunk effects; select on the shrunk ones. A condition that works in Asia for K7, P7 and U7 is one finding, not three weak ones.
E2. CLUSTER VOTES. One vote per redundancy cluster, equal weights; then learned weights with strong shrinkage toward equal. Compare: equal votes are harder to overfit.
E3. TRUE OUTCOMES FOR SIGNALS NOT TAKEN. Replace the hypothetical 1-minute bracket outcomes with engine outcomes: run each book ALONE in the exact engine with caps, stack rules and blackouts off ("isolation mode") so every signal has a real engine trade behind it. Use isolation outcomes for learning conditions, and the full stack engine for every verdict.
E4. ADAPTIVE WEIGHTS. Condition weights updated walk-forward from the last N weeks with decay (N = 13 / 26 / 52), and a simple bandit version that shifts weight toward conditions that have been right lately. Compare with fixed directions; report how fast weights move.
E5. ROBUSTNESS BATTERY for every finalist: thresholds moved +/-20%; each condition evaluated one bar late; one condition removed at a time; one whole cluster removed; data for one input missing (fallback = neutral). A checklist that breaks when one item is nudged is not a finding.

PART F — CONFLUENCE AND BRACKET JOINED (the two fixed tests are one idea: what do conditions say about THIS trade).
F1. ASYMMETRIC PER-TRADE BRACKET. From D6: a forecast of the favourable move sets the target, a forecast of the adverse move sets the stop, each from own-bar inputs plus the checklist; lots keep dollars at the stop constant; noise floor from the parent order (B2). Per book x session, clean cells first, then the assembled stack (B4).
F2. LEVEL-AWARE PLACEMENT. Target pulled to just before the nearest strong level cluster ahead when one sits within 0.75-1.25x the forecast target; stop pushed to just beyond a cluster behind when one sits within 1.25x the forecast stop (lots reduced to keep dollars equal). Separately and together with F1.
F3. TIME STOP FROM THE TIME-TO-TARGET FORECAST. Exit or tighten when the trade has taken k x its forecast time without reaching half the target (k = 1.5 / 2 / 3), per book.
F4. RE-BRACKET IN FLIGHT. Recompute the expected move every 5 minutes in the trade; tighten the target when the remaining expected move shrinks, never widen the stop. Jointly with the existing locks.
