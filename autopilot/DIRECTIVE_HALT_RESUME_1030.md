# T-HALT-RESUME — user directive (2026-10-30), re-sent 2026-10-31 (verbatim)

T-HALT-RESUME — not predicting, REACTING: halt a book or the stack when it is losing or the market is bad for it, keep scoring it on paper, resume when conditions come back (2026-10-30)

Research only. No live, execution-server or chart change. UNTOUCHED never read. Exact lock engine (halts interact with locks and re-entries); per book and stack; MEASURED fills; RECENT FIRST; session as a column; prereg per section; placebos; the QUANT-LAYER applies. Data: the stack's trade and 1-s series, the regime catalogue of T-TREND-GATE (+ addendum), the day-type tags, the book-defined vol regimes.

The question
Every program so far tries to PREDICT the next trade. This one does not: it watches what the strategy and the market have been DOING and switches books off when they are losing or when the regime is one they lose in, keeps a paper track running, and switches them back on when the paper track (or the regime) says conditions are back. The live week floor (−12R, off until Monday) is the crude version; nothing more sophisticated has been scored.

A. The halt triggers (each per book and for the stack; each with its own resume rule in C)
PERFORMANCE-BASED (the book's own recent results, realised): N consecutive losers (N = 3 / 4 / 5 / 6); rolling P&L over the last K trades below −X R (K = 5 / 10 / 20 / 30, X = 2 / 3 / 5); rolling 1-week / 2-week / 4-week P&L below −X R; drawdown from the book's rolling peak (20-trade / 1-week / 4-week peak) beyond X R; rolling win rate over K trades below the book's PRE median minus one SD; rolling profit factor < 0.8 / 1.0 over K; the book's day P&L below −X R (the live day floor at other levels); two / three losing days in a row; a losing week; the z-score of the last-K-trade mean vs the book's long-run distribution below −1 / −1.5 / −2 (the statistical "something changed").
EQUITY-CURVE RULES (classic): trade only when the book's equity curve is above its own 10 / 20 / 50-trade moving average (and the inverse, as a control); the equity curve's slope over K trades negative → halt; the "two-sigma band" halt (equity below the lower band of its rolling mean); the day-lock variants already live as references.
MARKET-CONDITION HALTS (the regime the book loses in, from the TREND-GATE map, NOT a forecast): halt the trend books when the realised measures say chop (Choppiness / ER / ADX / HMM chop state / variance ratio < 1 / negative autocorrelation) for ≥ 30 / 60 / 120 min; halt on a vol-regime mismatch (vol tercile the book loses in, from its own PRE+X' map); halt on the day type (the ex-ante tag) the book loses in; halt on cross-asset disagreement (ES/RTY against NQ for ≥ 30 min); halt on calendar (release windows, the days the book loses on by day of week from its own map); halt the stack when the "everything together" score is extreme (macro day) if the stack loses on those; halt a book when its MAE profile over the last K trades has shifted (deeper adverse excursions than its norm: the market is no longer giving its entries room).
COMBINED: performance AND regime (halt only when the book is losing AND the regime is its bad one — the case where the loss is explained, not noise); performance OR regime; the learned version: a walk-forward classifier of "the next K trades of this book will be net negative" from the recent-performance features + the regime features (no price forecast; only state), policy halt / half / full, 3-month weekly re-fit, shuffled-feature placebo; the extracted rules in words.

B. What happens while halted
Entries off for the book (or stack); open trades: let them run to their brackets vs flatten at the halt (both scored); the PAPER TRACK keeps scoring every signal the book would have taken (same fills, same brackets) so the resume rule has something to read; the lock engine treats halted books as absent (locks and re-entries real); lots freed by a halt: idle vs re-allocated to the books still on (risk-matched; reported as structure).

C. The resume rules (each × each halt trigger)
Time-based: resume at the next session boundary / next day / next Monday / after N trading days; paper-track-based: resume when the paper track shows N consecutive winners (2 / 3) / a positive last-K (5 / 10) / its equity back above its MA / a new paper-equity high; regime-based: resume when the regime measure that halted it has reversed for ≥ 30 / 60 / 120 min, or at the next trend-day tag; the asymmetric version (fast halt, slow resume; slow halt, fast resume); the "half size first" resume (resume at 50% lots, full after N paper/live winners).

D. Scoring
Stack weekly mean, SD, maxDD, worst-20-weeks, Sharpe; per book per contract; the time-halted share; the P&L the halt FORGOES (the paper track's P&L while halted, which is exactly what the rule gave up) beside the loss it AVOIDS; the equal-risk frame (a halt that cuts worst-20-weeks allows more lots; the factor stated, labelled structure); the lag cost (halts react late by construction: the mean P&L of the first K trades after a halt and after a resume); placebos: random halts at the same halted share and the same durations; shuffled-day performance series (the halt keyed to another book's results); the stack as it is and the live week floor as references; block-bootstrap CIs; RC per section; the drift of each trigger's value by quarter (does halting help more in 2025-26 than in 2023-24?); day-of-week and session splits; recent-first.
