# DS3_CONFLUENCE_L3_LIVE_SPEC (W-CF2, updated 2026-11-15): what the VPS sidecar must compute live for the L3 lead, what it needs, and what it costs
VERDICT (data ends 2026-10-30): the lead (own-side order adds minus opposite-side adds over the last 30-60 s, low versus the previous 20 trading days) needs live market-by-order (L3) data for the NQ front month and nothing else; it survives the value being 1, 2 and 5 seconds late (lift $144 at 0 s, $147 at 1 s, $144 at 2 s, $158 at 5 s, weekly-cluster t 3.4-3.8, all above the nonsense 99th-percentile bar 2.7), an L2 depth proxy does NOT reproduce it (lift +$12), and the work for a Python sidecar is small (about 150-400 records/s on average, 1,000-2,300/s at the 99th percentile, 12,000/s peak; far below one core with a compiled kernel). The budget figure for the feed is $1,750 per month (Databento Plus as the user quoted), possibly $3,500 if the CME licence is billed on top, until Databento confirms in writing; the cheapest alternative to verify is a Rithmic route at roughly $150-250 per month. FIRST PASS, nothing is a PASS; the exact engine decides (W-CF3 sets TL3_CF_*).

## 1. Inputs (exact)
- Stream: CME MDP 3.0 market-by-order for ONE instrument, the front-month NQ contract D7 trades (same roll rule as the engine). Databento live: dataset GLBX.MDP3, schema `mbo`, one raw symbol (e.g. NQZ6) or the instrument id. Per record: ts_recv, action (A add, M modify, C cancel, F fill, T trade, R clear), side (B/A), price, size, order_id, flags. Any equivalent L3 feed works if it exposes these fields.
- Book state kept in the sidecar: order table order_id -> (price tick = price/0.25, size, side); best bid and best ask in ticks after every record. Rules (copied from code/mbo_book, tested against 1,126 days): A inserts; C removes `size` (whole order if size 0 or too big); F reduces the resting order; M replaces price and size; R clears everything; T never changes the book.
- Counters per side per second: ADD_NEAR[side][second] = number of records with action A whose price is within 5 ticks of the best price OF THE SAME SIDE at that instant (before insertion). Order COUNT, not size. (A size version, contracts added, is a close substitute: correlation 0.95.)

## 2. The value and the yes/no at a decision second T for side s (+1 buy, -1 sell)
- Window = the W complete seconds [T-1-lag-W+1, T-1-lag] ending the second BEFORE T (nothing from second T). Base reading W = 60, lag = 0. A stronger variant is W = 30 (see DS3_CONFLUENCE_L3_LEAD.md, section 3).
- own = sum of ADD_NEAR on the trade's own side over the window (bids for a buy, asks for a sell), opp = the other side. value = (own - opp) / max(own + opp, 1).
- 20-day reference: the sidecar stores `value` for EVERY entry signal D7 evaluates (all books, taken and skipped, all sessions) with its engine trading day. For a signal on trading day d: take all stored values from the 20 trading days before d (d excluded), require at least 150, q20 = linear-interpolated 20th percentile (numpy default). Yes = value <= q20 (about 20% of entries); the mirror/veto reading uses cancel-to-trade (cancelled contracts within 5 ticks on the own side over contracts traded against own side + 1, 60 s) above its 80th percentile.
- Why a percentile reference: order sizes at the touch, cancel-to-trade and spoof-like frequency drifted strongly between 2023 and 2026, so fixed thresholds do not carry across years; the percentile form does (positive in 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026).
- Warm-up: 20 trading days of the same stored values before the first usable day; before that the item is unknown. Unknown rule used in the tests: keep the signal (no gate). The sidecar should publish `unknown` explicitly (feed down, fewer than 150 reference values, a gap above 5 s inside the window).

## 3. Latency tolerance (scored on the 19,798 taken trades, whole reading incl. its reference shifted; DS3_CONFLUENCE_L3_LEAD.md section 4)
| value computed | lift $/trade (base lots) | weekly-cluster t | beats nonsense p99 (2.7) |
|--|--|--|--|
| at the signal second (0 s) | 144 | 3.39 | yes |
| 1 s late | 147 | 3.46 | yes |
| 2 s late | 144 | 3.38 | yes |
| 5 s late | 158 | 3.81 | yes |
So the sidecar may deliver the value up to 5 s after the signal second without losing the effect; the 60 s window means one second of lateness changes about 1.7% of its content (correlation of the value at lag 5 with lag 0 is 0.92). Practical rule: publish a fresh value every second and let D7 read the latest one.

## 4. Message rate, CPU and memory (measured on 60 sampled days of the NQ front month)
- Records per day 11-33 million; mean 146-372 per active second; 99th percentile 1,000-2,300 per second; maximum 5,800-12,200 per second (cash-open bursts).
- Our compiled kernel (numba) processes a whole 24-hour day, including decoding the file, in about 15 s on one core, i.e. well under 1% of one core at real-time rates; a pure-Python event loop at 2-10 microseconds per record would use about 1-5% of a core on average and 10-25% at the peak second, so use numba/Cython or a C extension anyway for headroom.
- Memory: order table 4 million slots (about 120 MB); one float per second of counters; a few MB for the 20-day reference. No raw records need to be stored.
- Output to D7: one line per second {ts, value_60, value_30, ctr_mine_60, yes_lead, yes_mirror, unknown} to a file or local socket, 1 Hz.

## 5. Feed requirement and cost: reconciling $199 and $1,750
- Sources in our files: (a) DS3_TOOLS_T1_P3.md (fetched 2026-11-13, Databento pricing page and blog): Standard $199/month ($179 if grandfathered) but L2/L3 appear to have been REMOVED from Standard (user report on Databento's roadmap site); Plus = $1,750 plan fee + $1,750 CME licence fee per month (annual contract, contact sales, about $3,500); Unlimited $4,500 + $4,500; usage-based live CME discontinued since 2025-04-16. (b) My earlier quote page DS3_CONFLUENCE_L3_QUOTES.md (pricing-page summary 2026-11-14): Standard $199 with "no license fees", Plus "$1,750 in license fees per month", Unlimited "$4,500", MBO live listed but its plan column unclear. (c) Today's Databento docs (databento.com/docs/portal/live-data): real-time data is "included with a Standard, Plus, or Unlimited plan"; CME licence for personal (non-professional) use "included with Standard plan (up to 2 devices)", professional $973 per exchange; the page does not map MBO to a plan. (d) Our own entitlement test (DS3_LIVE_MBO.md): the account's live plan today is L1-only (no MBO, no MBP-10).
- Reconciliation: $199 is Standard, which our account has and which does not give live MBO (user's statement and our test agree); $1,750 is the Plus tier, the one that lists L3 live; the pages disagree on whether $1,750 is the plan fee or only the licence fee (so $1,750 or $3,500 per month). Neither number is confirmed in writing. Single figure to budget: $1,750 per month (Plus), with a stated ceiling of $3,500 until Databento sales answer in writing which plan carries live MBO and what the CME licence adds for a personal account.
- Cheapest alternative to check: Rithmic via the user's FCM. Public sources (AMP, Ironbeam, Bookmap, searched 2026-11-15): R|API+ about $100-125 per month, CME depth fees about $12-48 per month non-professional (professional much higher), Bookmap resells Rithmic CME MBO at $39 per exchange or $101 for the CME bundle; I found no confirmation that Rithmic's order-by-order data is exposed to a user's own code at those prices. CQG reportedly has no MBO; dxFeed reportedly none. If Rithmic MBO via R|API+ is real, the all-in cost is about $150-250 per month but D7's sidecar would have to speak the Rithmic protocol instead of Databento's. Needs one email to the FCM; not done (no spend, no signup).
- What the money buys, in table terms: the lead is worth +$144 per trade on 3,861 yes-trades of 19,798 (about 4 a day) at base lots, +$252 at x1.75, positive in each of 2023-26; as a skip gate it keeps 20% of trades with $222 per trade versus $106 (max drawdown in trade order 44.9k versus 118.6k); as a size rule (x1.5 yes) the total rises 20% with more drawdown. Whether any of that survives the exact engine is the open question.
