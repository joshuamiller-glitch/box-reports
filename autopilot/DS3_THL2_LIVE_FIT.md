## 1. Answer
1. **The evaluation clock is what matters.** Evaluating the stored rule (adverse <= -30 AND 5-s pulled support >= 30 x median level, first row) on EVERY SECOND instead of the stored 10-s grid raises cut/no-cut agreement with live from 128/186 to 170/186 trades (informative trades, those that reached -25: fit half 31/62 -> 55/62, held-out half 27/53 -> 45/53). A grid anchored at the entry second (10-s) is about the same as the epoch grid (best fit agreement of any setting: entry-anchored 44, epoch 43, 1-s 56). Live cuts 45 informative trades that a 10-s grid never sees (short wicks through -30 between grid rows): this is the live-cut / engine-not side of BASE-4's table, not the engine-cut / live-held side.
2. **Selected rule on the FIT half** (maximum informative agreement 56/62; 84 of 129,600 variants tie, fewest changes from the default chosen): clock 1 s, persistence 1, arm 30 on the last print, MULT 60, window 3 s, all-level pulled, 10-level median, no minimum age, no thick-book condition. Held-out (Wed-Thu): 45/53 informative (6 false cuts, 2 missed) against the default's 27/53; all 186 trades 171/186; on the 122 live trades that have an engine counterpart 113/122 (default 84/122). Rule is 'consistent with live' by the prereg test (held-out agreement above the default, held-out false cuts equal to the default's 6, not larger).
3. **What does NOT matter in this week's data:** persistence (>= 2 or 3 rows is slightly worse on the 1-s clock), the multiplier (20 to 100 give the same answer; the liquidity condition is satisfied on almost every armed row, medians 137-205 times the median level), pulled-liquidity depth (level 1 vs 3 vs 10 levels) and median depth (1, 3, 5, 10 levels), the 10-level thick-book condition, a minimum age up to 10 s. Arm 35 and 40 are clearly worse (fit 36, 27 vs 56): the arm is -30 (25 about the same, see the table).
4. **The engine-cut / live-held pairs (8) are NOT explained by any variant in the prereg space.** The selected rule predicts a cut on 5 of those 8 live trades (the default also 5): they are trades that went to -38 to -82 points with a huge pulled-support ratio and live did not cut (two ran to their stops, K7 -83 and M7 -80.75; three later hit the 60-pt target; the other three of the 8 never reached -30: -26.9, -26.3, -2.9). The same holds for the 12 live trades that reached -30 and were not cut (88 were): see section 4. The engine variant with a 1-s decision clock keeps 8 of 8 false cuts (section 5).

## 3. How the trigger series were built and checked
Per live trade, 1-s grid from entry+2 s to the live exit second (P2 table: price = last print forward-filled, alternative worst print of the second; supporting side = bid for longs; p = cancel size any level on that side over the w bins ending at T; median = median of the 10 own-side levels; adverse from the live average entryPx). Two checks of the reconstruction on the ENGINE side: (a) the default 10-s rule evaluated on the 182 sealed engine trades with their own entry prices reproduces the engine cut second exactly (median time difference 0 s, q05 -0.6 s) on 30 of the cuts it predicts, but misses 55 of the 85 engine cuts because the engine measures the adverse from the library entry E while the sealed csv carries the filled average price (engine entries are 3.4-5.3 pt better than the touch); with the engine reference price the stored K rows tie: K.p5 equals the table 5-s cancel sum on 84% of the blind-span decision rows (all rows of Sun-Wed, differences of +-1 to 5 contracts on part of Thu 11-05), K.med equals the table median of the same second on 100%, K.upnl equals side x (path price of the second before the row - E) on 100%, the cut delta formula (upnl - 0.995 - (pts_lib - fill cost)) on 100% of 27,875 rows. (b) the engine injection of section 5 reproduces the sealed list to the dollar (+34,879, 182 trades) before any change.

## 6. Hypotheses for the user's code/log check (one week; each is a question, not a result)
* H1: the live TH-L2 is evaluated on a faster clock than the stored 10-s grid (1 s or per book event). Check the per-cut log's evaluation timestamps: the D7_LOGGING_REQUEST.md th_cuts.csv columns `armed_ts_ms` (first time the trade reached the -30 arm), `cut_trigger_ts_ms` and `rule_params` (`A30|k30|10s|medlvl`: is the 10 s the evaluation period or the pulled-size window?), and the code that schedules the TH-L2 evaluation (timer period / OnMarketDepth / OnBarUpdate).

* H4: pulled-liquidity units.
