## 4. The FVG lead in detail

**Rule (NT8-codable).** NQ, 5-minute bars clock-aligned from the 18:00 ET session open, ATR(14) Wilder. On the close of each 5-min bar t, if flat and no order pending: bullish imbalance if Low[t] > High[t-2] and Low[t] - High[t-2] >= 0.25 x ATR[t] (bearish mirror). Place a limit buy at CE = (High[t-2] + Low[t]) / 2 (limit sell for the bearish case), good for 20 bars (100 min), cancel on expiry; stop = Low[t-2] - 1 tick (High[t-2] + 1 tick), moved out to 0.75 ATR from the entry if closer, trade skipped if the stop is > 4 ATR, < 6 pts or > 150 pts away; target = entry +- 2 x stop distance; flatten at 16:55 ET, maximum hold 360 min; no entries 16:55-18:00 ET; all sessions; one position at a time. Size at library standard: contracts = min(24, 9,600 / (stop pts x 20)); with the volatility-matched factor 0.81 and a 20% share the average is 3.3 and the maximum 3.9 NQ contracts (MNQ gives the granularity). Averages: 12.4 trades per day, stop 24 pts, median hold 24 min, win rate 37.9%, +3.2 pts per trade net of costs. This needs a new NT8 strategy (ATM bracket plus a managed limit order with an expiry); DualStrat is untouched. NQ intraday positions net against ST/HM, so it must sit in a separate account (library class NQ_INTRADAY). Margin is not verified against the brokerage schedule: at 20% share about 4 NQ contracts worst case, flat by 16:55 ET; entries are possible in Globex hours, so check whether the brokerage's intraday margin applies outside RTH.

**Mechanism (one sentence).** A quick three-bar displacement that leaves an unfilled imbalance is followed, on a 2-15 minute horizon, by continuation after a retrace to the midpoint of the gap: short-horizon order-flow persistence (the DRIVERS finding) harvested with a resting limit order and a stop beyond the origin candle. **Why this is only a LEAD, and what could still be wrong.**
1. Sizing convention: the plateau gate fails by one neighbour under the pre-registered R-sized sleeve and passes under the vol-matched convention; both margins are tiny (0.501 vs 0.515, 0.439 vs 0.435). Lead's call.
2. Fill realism is the whole question. 12 trades per day with resting limits at the gap midpoint; the simulation fills whenever the 1-minute row trades one tick through, with no queue or priority loss. The most pessimistic row (2-tick trade-through, +1 tick entry slippage, 2x stop slippage) still gives +0.13 / +0.29 / +0.52 uplift, but tick-level adverse selection of passive orders is exactly what an OHLC simulation cannot see. The edge falls with order latency: entry orders placed 1 / 2 / 5 minutes late give all-in standalone Sharpe 2.98 / 2.78 / 1.93 (clean 2.42 / 2.11 / 1.12) vs 3.18 at zero lag.
3. ES replicates only at tf 5 and 15 (the tick is a larger share of the stop); RTY replicates at tf 3-5. NQ has the lowest tick-to-stop ratio of the three.
4. The 54 selected cells are one effect, not 54 independent finds; the sibling choice (tf3 vs tf5, CE vs far edge) was made on the full gate table, not pre-declared. Family-level evidence is stronger than the single-cell evidence.
5. The quoted Sharpes (2.7-3.6 clean) are weekly Sharpes of a sleeve scaled by contracts; the dollars at standard size (+$5.4M vol-matched all-in) are not an account projection. At a 20% share the sleeve is about 3.3 contracts; expect the live Sharpe below the simulated one.
