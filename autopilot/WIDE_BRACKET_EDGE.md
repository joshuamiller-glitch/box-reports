## Why the two tables disagreed (found and checked by re-running each null in the other kernel)
1. **Null definition (the main cause).** W-SUNDAY-WB draws keep the SAME DAY as the real trade, randomise the entry second inside the book's own window and permute the side labels (drift null), with the real trade being the EXACT ENGINE result. W-PLAN-AS drew a RANDOM DAY and a uniform time in the whole session window with the real side per slot. They test different things: the monkey that knows nothing (mine) versus a monkey that knows which days the book trades (theirs). For a two-sided book the same-day null is the stricter bar and fails when the book's edge is mostly WHICH DAYS it trades (day selection): Asia MF1 short 72%/81%, Asia MF3 short 55%, NY AM MF2 short 76%, NY PM MF1 short 52%, London MF3 short 66%/90%. For a LONG-ONLY book the same-day null is biased towards the draws (a long-only monkey on up days earns the day's drift; its win is not an entry skill): that is why NY AM MF6, MF7, MF9 get 0-12% in the W-SUNDAY table and 94-98% in mine.
2. **Time of day (a real gap in my first version).** My draws were uniform over the whole session window, so a book that trades at a time with a systematic move (the first hour of Asia, the NY open) was compared with the wrong monkey. FIX: the new primary null is the TIME-OF-DAY MATCHED monkey: random day (any session day), entry time-of-day resampled from the book's own real entries, side labels permuted among the book's trades, 200 draws.
3. **Real = engine vs bare.** W-SUNDAY-WB compares the exact engine trades with bare draws (engine includes the live exits: PolicyTake, EarlyCut, locks, DayHalt); I compared bare re-runs of the same trades with bare draws (same exits on both sides). W-SUNDAY-WB's own entries-only replay column removes this difference and agrees with my bare numbers (e.g. NY AM MF4 short 90%, my kernel under their null 95%). The engine-vs-bare column below shows where the live exits eat the entry edge (Asia MF4: bare short +$98k, engine +$14k).
4. **Costs and window.** Mine: 0.24 pt fee + 0.25 pt stop slip per trade; theirs: $4.84 per lot and no slippage; the draw window differs (0.5-99.5 percentile of the book's own offsets vs the full session). Not enough to flip a verdict by themselves (checked: my kernel under THEIR null reproduces W-SUNDAY-WB's entries-only beat within 10 points for 34 of 34 book-sides, and my engine-trade comparison under their null reproduces their reported beat for 30 of 34; the 4 exceptions are engine-vs-bare ones).
5. **Week definition.** Same Monday-anchored session weeks (NQ down weeks 74-78, up 107); my earlier down-week figures were pts per trade, theirs are $ per week: the page uses THEIR $/week table (the pts version is in the appendix).
6. **Verdict thresholds.** Mine required beat >= 0.95 overall, >= 0.90 per side and a down-week gain of 0.5 pt; theirs >= 0.90 on each side of the same-day null. Reconciled rule below.

**RECONCILED VERDICT (rule written before the table): EDGE BEYOND DRIFT = (a) beats >= 90% of the 200 TIME-OF-DAY-MATCHED monkey draws on EACH side the book trades, AND (b) in NQ-down weeks the real result per week is above the monkey's (W-SUNDAY-WB $/wk table), AND (c) the engine result is positive. Flags: DAY-SELECTION = fails the same-day null (<90% on a side) i.e. the edge is partly which days it trades; TREND-DEPENDENT = long-only book that loses in NQ-down weeks; EXITS-ERASE = engine side result < 30% of the bare side result.**
